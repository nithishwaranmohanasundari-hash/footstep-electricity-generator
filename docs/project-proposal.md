FOOTSTEP-BASED ENERGY HARVESTING SYSTEM
1. Project Title

Footstep-Based Energy Harvesting and Storage System Using Magnetic Induction

GitHub Repository Name
footstep-electricity-generator
2. Abstract

The Footstep-Based Energy Harvesting System is a renewable-energy prototype designed to convert mechanical energy produced by human footsteps into electrical energy.

The system uses a flexible top platform, springs, triangular supporting structures, magnets, copper coils, a rectifier, a charging/control circuit, and a rechargeable battery.

When a person steps on the flexible platform, mechanical pressure causes controlled downward movement. The spring mechanism controls the movement and returns the platform to its original position. The movement of the magnetic system produces a change in magnetic flux through the copper winding, generating an electrical output.

The generated electrical output is processed through a rectifier and suitable voltage/charging control circuit before being stored in a rechargeable battery. The stored energy can subsequently be used for suitable low-power applications such as LEDs, sensors, displays, and IoT devices.

The proposed system can be installed in locations with high pedestrian traffic, including schools, colleges, offices, shopping centers, and public buildings.

3. Introduction

A large amount of mechanical energy is produced whenever people walk. Normally, this energy is dissipated into the ground and is not utilized.

The objective of this project is to investigate whether a portion of this mechanical energy can be captured through a specially designed footstep platform and converted into electrical energy.

The proposed platform contains multiple mechanical generation units. When a person steps on the platform, the flexible structure moves within a controlled range. Springs provide mechanical support and help maintain the platform's position.

The magnetic movement produces changing magnetic flux associated with the copper winding. According to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, a changing magnetic flux can induce an electromotive force in a conductor.

The generated electrical energy can then be conditioned and stored for later use.

4. Problem Statement

Conventional walking surfaces allow the mechanical energy generated by footsteps to be dissipated without useful recovery.

There is therefore an opportunity to develop a system that:

captures mechanical energy from footsteps,
converts it into electrical energy,
stores the generated energy,
and makes the stored energy available for small electrical applications.

7. Mechanical Design

The platform consists of several important sections.

7.1 Flexible Top Layer

A flexible but mechanically durable material forms the upper surface.

This is the part on which a person steps.

The platform should:

support the expected load,
flex within a controlled range,
return to its original position,
withstand repeated operation.
7.2 Ground/Base Layer

A rigid base is fixed to the ground.

It supports:

springs,
triangular structures,
magnetic mechanism,
electrical components.
7.3 Controlled Gap

A controlled gap is maintained between the upper and lower structures.

For the initial concept, approximately 5 cm can be considered as a design reference, but the final gap must be determined experimentally based on:

spring compression,
magnet travel,
maximum user load,
structural strength,
required electrical output.

The platform should never allow the magnets to mechanically collide.

8. Spring Mechanism

Springs are positioned around the central generation mechanism.

Their functions are:

Mechanical support

They support the top platform.

Movement control

They limit excessive downward movement.

Restoration

After the person removes their foot, the springs push the platform back toward its original position.

Balance

Multiple springs distributed around the platform help maintain stability.

When foot pressure is applied, the upper mechanical section moves.

This produces relative movement in the magnetic system.

The changing magnetic field/flux associated with the copper winding is used to generate electrical output.

10. Copper Winding

Copper wire is used as the electrical conductor for electromagnetic induction.

The winding contains many turns of insulated copper wire.

The number of turns, wire diameter, coil dimensions, magnet strength, and relative movement all affect the generated voltage.

The coil output is connected to the external electrical circuit through wires.

Important engineering point

The final prototype must be designed so that there is meaningful relative magnetic flux change through the coil. Simply wrapping a coil around a magnet and moving the entire coil and magnet together does not automatically produce useful electrical energy.

This should therefore be validated experimentally during prototype development.

11. Electromagnetic Induction

The principle is based on Faraday's law:

$$ E=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt} $$

Where:

\(E\) = induced electromotive force
\(N\) = number of turns in the coil
\(\Phi\) = magnetic flux
\(t\) = time

Therefore, a greater rate of change of magnetic flux can produce a greater induced voltage.

In the proposed project:

Footstep
   ↓
Mechanical movement
   ↓
Magnetic flux changes
   ↓
Voltage induced in coil
   ↓
Electrical output

17. Working Process
Step 1 — Footstep

A person steps on the flexible top platform.

Step 2 — Mechanical compression

The top platform moves downward against the spring mechanism.

Step 3 — Magnetic movement

The mechanical movement produces relative movement in the magnetic generation mechanism.

Step 4 — Electrical generation

The changing magnetic flux induces voltage in the copper winding.

Step 5 — AC output

The generator produces an electrical output that can be measured.

Step 6 — Rectification

A bridge rectifier converts the generated AC into DC.

Step 7 — Regulation

The DC output is filtered and controlled to provide a suitable charging input.

Step 8 — Battery storage

The electrical energy is stored in the rechargeable battery.

Step 9 — Usage

The stored energy can be supplied to a suitable low-power load.

Project Core Idea
Footstep-Based Energy Harvesting and Storage System

The core idea of this project is to convert the mechanical energy produced by human footsteps into electrical energy and store it for later use.

The system consists of a flexible top platform and a fixed bottom platform, supported by springs and structural supports. A controlled gap is maintained between the platforms so that the top platform can move downward when a person steps on it without excessive compression.

At the center of the mechanism, two magnets are positioned opposite each other, with the electromagnetic generation arrangement using insulated copper windings. When a person steps on the platform, the applied force causes controlled mechanical movement, changing the magnetic flux associated with the winding and producing an electrical output through electromagnetic induction.

The generated electrical output is then passed through a bridge rectifier, which converts the AC output into DC. A suitable filtering, voltage-regulation, and battery-charging circuit is then used to safely store the generated energy in a rechargeable battery.
